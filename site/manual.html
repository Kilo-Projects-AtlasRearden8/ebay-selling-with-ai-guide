Welcome. This manual walks you through using free AI to sell on eBay: writing titles, descriptions, prepping photos, pricing, shipping policies, and replying to buyers. No paid tools required.

1. Free AI tools (no paid tools needed)

You can run this entire eBay AI workflow with free tools. You do not need a subscription.

Pick one to start. Google AI Studio and Bing Copilot work in your browser with no install. If you want privacy first, try Ollama on your own computer.

Sample first prompt
I’m selling a used [item name, e.g. men’s Nike running shoes, size 10] on eBay. The condition is [excellent/good/fair] and it has [any flaws, e.g. slight scuff on left heel]. Write a friendly, honest eBay title (80 characters or less) and a short description (about 3 short paragraphs) that mentions the brand, size, condition, and what’s included. Do not make up facts.
Good at: drafting titles, descriptions, policies, messages. Not good at: knowing your item’s true condition, exact sold prices, or guaranteeing a sale. Always double-check AI output.

2. Writing eBay titles

Your title is the first thing buyers see. It needs to say what the item is, the brand, the size or model, and the condition — all within eBay’s 80-character limit.

What makes a good title

  • Brand name (if known)
  • Item name and model
  • Size, color, or key specs
  • Condition (new, like new, good, etc.)
  • What’s included — only if it fits
  • No all-caps, no excessive exclamation points, no misleading words
AI prompt for titles
I’m selling [item name] on eBay. Brand: [brand or “unknown”]. Model/size: [size]. Condition: [condition]. Includes: [what’s included, or “nothing extra”]. Write 3 eBay title options, each 80 characters or fewer. Be specific and honest. Do not exaggerate the condition or include brand names I didn’t list.
Example: used iPad Air 3, 64 GB, Wi-Fi, good condition, with charger. AI might suggest: “Apple iPad Air 3 64GB Wi-Fi Good Condition w/ Charger”
Count the characters (eBay caps titles at 80). Make sure every claim matches the item’s real condition.

3. Writing eBay descriptions

The description builds trust. A good one tells the buyer exactly what they’re getting, so there are fewer questions and fewer returns.

A good description includes

  • What the item is (brand, model, size, color)
  • Condition and any flaws (be honest — photos should match)
  • What’s included in the sale
  • Measurements or specs, if relevant
  • Shipping speed and carrier
  • Return policy (if you offer returns)
AI prompt for descriptions
I’m selling [item name] on eBay. Brand: [brand]. Model/size: [size]. Color: [color]. Condition: [condition], and here are the flaws: [flaws or “none visible”]. Includes: [what’s included]. I ship within [1–3] business days via [carrier]. Returns: [accepted/not accepted, within X days]. Write a warm, honest eBay description in about 4 short paragraphs. Use short sentences and bullet lists where helpful. Do not invent features or guarantees.
Use bullet points for condition and what’s included — buyers skim. Mention flaws up front; honest listings get fewer disputes.

4. Photos

Great photos sell. AI can’t take the photos for you, but it can help you plan what to shoot and suggest simple free edits.

AI prompt to plan your photo list
I’m selling [item name], [brand], [condition]. What photos should I take for an eBay listing? List 6–8 specific shots, including close-ups of any flaws, labels, serial numbers, and what’s included.

Shoot with what you have

  • A clean, well-lit spot near a window (daylight works great)
  • A plain background — a white sheet or poster board
  • Your phone camera. Wipe the lens first.
  • Take more photos than you think you need; delete later.

Edit for free

Your phone’s built-in editor: crop, straighten, adjust brightness. Don’t over-edit — photos must still look like the real item.

Privacy: Don’t upload photos showing your face, home address, mail, kids, or anything identifying to a free AI tool.

5. Pricing

AI can help you think through a price, but free tools can’t look up live eBay sold prices reliably. Use AI to organize your research, then set the price yourself.

Research a price (free)

  1. Search eBay for your item, then filter to Sold listings.
  2. Note what similar items actually sold for.
  3. Compare condition — “like new” sells for more than “fair.”
  4. Check how many are selling and how fast.
AI prompt for pricing
I’m selling [item name], [brand], [model], [condition]. I found these recent sold prices on eBay: [list a few, e.g. $25, $30, $22]. It includes [what’s included]. Suggest a starting price and a “buy it now” price, and explain why.
Price slightly below similar sold items to sell faster. Factor in shipping cost — decide whether you charge shipping or offer free shipping.

6. Shipping policies

Your shipping policy tells buyers how fast you ship, which carrier you use, and whether you accept returns. Clear policies reduce questions and disputes.

Decide before listing

  • Handling time (how soon you ship after purchase)
  • Carrier (USPS, UPS, FedEx) and service
  • Who pays return shipping, if you accept returns
  • Free shipping or charge the buyer
AI prompt for shipping policy
I sell on eBay. I ship within [1–3] business days via [USPS/UPS/FedEx]. I [do/do not] offer free shipping. Returns: [accepted within X days, buyer pays return shipping / not accepted]. Write a short, friendly eBay shipping and return policy in 2–3 short paragraphs. Be clear and honest. Do not promise anything I can’t deliver.
Only promise handling times you can keep. Use tracking for anything over ~$25.

7. Buyer messages

Quick, polite replies keep buyers happy and protect your seller rating. AI can draft replies for common situations.

Before buying (question)
A buyer is asking: “[their question].” The item is [item], [condition], [price], ships within [X] days via [carrier]. Write a short, friendly eBay reply answering the question and inviting them to buy. Don’t make up facts.
After purchase (thank you)
Write a friendly eBay message to a buyer who just bought [item]. Thank them, confirm it will ship within [X] business days via [carrier], and say they’ll get a tracking number. Keep it under 4 sentences.
Shipping delay
Write a polite eBay message telling a buyer their item will ship [X] days later than expected because [reason]. Apologize briefly, give the new ship date, and thank them for patience.
Return request
A buyer wants to return [item] because [reason]. My policy is [your policy]. Write a courteous eBay reply that explains next steps and follows my policy. Stay calm and professional.
Reply within 24 hours when you can. Never share personal info — use eBay’s messaging system.

8. Privacy rules for eBay sellers using AI

Free AI tools are helpful, but you must protect your personal information and your buyers’ information. Treat every AI prompt as if it could be read by others.

Never put these into a free AI tool

  • Your real name, home address, phone number, or email
  • eBay username or password
  • Bank account, credit card, or PayPal details
  • Social Security number or tax ID
  • Buyer names, addresses, or order details
  • Photos showing your face, your home, mail, kids, or anything identifying
Safe practice: use placeholders
I’m selling [item], [brand], [condition]. I ship within [X] days via [carrier]. Returns [accepted/not accepted].
For maximum privacy, use a local AI tool (e.g., Ollama) that runs on your own computer — nothing leaves your machine.
Don’t copy a buyer’s address or personal details into an AI tool. Reply through eBay’s messaging system and use placeholders instead.
