# 4. Photos with AI

Great photos sell. AI can't take the photos for you, but it can help you plan what to shoot, write photo descriptions, and suggest simple edits you can do with free tools.

## Plan your photo list with AI

Ask AI what photos a buyer of your item would want to see:

> I'm selling [item name], [brand], [condition]. What photos should I take for an eBay listing? List 6–8 specific shots, including close-ups of any flaws, labels, serial numbers, and what's included.

Example result for a camera:
- Front view of the camera body
- Top with dials and buttons
- Back screen
- Lens, front and back
- Bottom with battery and serial number label
- Strap and charger (what's included)
- Close-up of the scuff on the side (flaw)
- Size reference next to a common object (optional)

## Shoot with what you have

- A clean, well-lit spot near a window (daylight works great).
- A plain background — a white sheet or poster board.
- Your phone camera. Wipe the lens first.
- Take more photos than you think you need; delete later.

## Edit for free

- Your phone's built-in editor: crop, straighten, adjust brightness.
- Free web editors (e.g., your OS photos app) — no install needed.
- Don't over-edit: photos must still look like the real item.

## Writing photo captions/alt text with AI

> Write short, honest alt-text captions for these eBay photos of a [item]: [list the photos]. Keep each caption under 20 words, mention flaws where visible, and don't exaggerate.

## Privacy

Don't upload photos that show your face, home address, mail, kids, or anything identifying to a free AI tool. If using a local AI tool (Ollama), photos stay on your computer — see [Privacy rules](08-privacy.md).
