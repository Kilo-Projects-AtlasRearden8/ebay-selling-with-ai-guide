# 2. Writing eBay titles with AI

Your title is the first thing buyers see. It needs to say what the item is, the brand, the size or model, and the condition — all within eBay's 80-character limit.

## What makes a good eBay title

- Brand name (if known)
- Item name and model
- Size, color, or key specs
- Condition (new, like new, good, etc.)
- What's included (charger, box, etc.) — only if it fits
- No all-caps, no excessive exclamation points, no misleading words

## Free AI prompt for titles

> I'm selling [item name] on eBay. Brand: [brand or "unknown"]. Model/size: [size]. Condition: [condition]. Includes: [what's included, or "nothing extra"]. Write 3 eBay title options, each 80 characters or fewer. Be specific and honest. Do not exaggerate the condition or include brand names I didn't list. List each title on its own line.

## Example

You're selling a used iPad:

> I'm selling an Apple iPad on eBay. Model: iPad Air, 3rd generation, 64 GB, Wi-Fi only. Condition: good — screen has a few light scratches, works perfectly. Includes: tablet and charging cable, no box. Write 3 eBay title options, each 80 characters or fewer...

Possible AI results:
1. Apple iPad Air 3 64GB Wi-Fi Good Condition w/ Charger
2. iPad Air 3rd Gen 64GB Wi-Fi Tablet + Charger, Good
3. Apple iPad Air 3, 64GB, Wi-Fi, Good Condition, Charger Included

Pick the one that fits your item best, then trim it to 80 characters if needed.

## Before you list

- Count the characters (eBay caps titles at 80).
- Make sure every claim matches the item's real condition.
- Avoid words you aren't sure about (e.g., "rare," "vintage," "authentic") unless you can back them up.
