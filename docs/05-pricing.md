# 5. Pricing with AI

AI can help you think through a price, but it can't look up live eBay sold prices for you (free tools don't browse current sales reliably). Use AI to organize your research, then set the price yourself.

## How to research a price (free)

1. Search eBay for your item, then filter to **Sold listings**.
2. Note what similar items actually sold for (not what they're listed for).
3. Compare condition — a "like new" item sells for more than "fair."
4. Check how many are selling and how fast.

## Free AI prompt for pricing

> I'm selling [item name], [brand], [model], [condition], [age if known]. I found these recent sold prices on eBay: [list a few, e.g. $25, $30, $22]. It includes [what's included]. Suggest a starting price and a "buy it now" price, and explain why. Keep it simple.

AI will reason through condition, included accessories, and demand. You make the final call.

## Pricing tips

- Price slightly below similar sold items to sell faster.
- If it's not selling after 2–3 weeks, consider lowering by 10–15%.
- Factor in shipping cost — decide whether you charge shipping or offer free shipping.
- Don't price based on what you paid; price based on what it's worth now.

## Example output

> Starting price: $22 (auction-style, attracts bidders)
> Buy It Now: $29 (fair for good condition, includes charger)
> Why: similar units sold $20–$32; yours includes the charger, which adds value.
